Saari’s conjecture for four equal masses

In the planar Newtonian problem, constant moment of inertia about the center of mass forces a collision-free solution to rotate uniformly as a fixed shape.

What the animation shows

The two motions start from the same equilateral triangle with a fourth mass at its center. The left trajectory is an exact relative equilibrium. The right is a numerical integration from certified initial data with seven zero inertia derivatives and a positive eighth derivative.

Proof chapters in text
Dimension three · A rectangle on the level
The parallelogram sweep traces a family of shapes at fixed potential. The proof places a nonsquare rectangle in the required component of the full potential level, then excludes every complex velocity direction there.
Dimension two · A tangential divisor
A complementary partial potential must have a pole on the complex phase surface. Transverse arcs are excluded. At infinity the selected divisor has a star of maximal poles or four maximal poles with adjacent zeros. At finite shape four zero graphs remain. Local equations exclude all six possibilities.
Dimension one · A conic obstruction
If every finite zero belonged to a complementary double zero, the normalized pair sums would lie on a conic. The planar Gram determinant would make a product a square, but that product has a simple zero. A finite nontrue triangle is forced, and its four profiles are excluded.
